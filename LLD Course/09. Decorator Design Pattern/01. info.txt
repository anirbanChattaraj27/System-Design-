IS-A Relationship (Inheritance):
    The IS-A relationship represents a hierarchy where a subclass "is a type of" its superclass.
        1. Purpose: Used for specialization and code reuse. The subclass inherits all public and protected properties and behaviors of the parent.
        2. Example: A SavingsAccount IS-A BankAccount. It shares all basic banking functions but may add specific features like interest calculation.
        3. When to use: Use inheritance only when there is a natural hierarchical relationship where the subclass can be used anywhere the parent is expected in Liskov Substitution Principle

HAS-A Relationship (Composition & Aggregation):
    The HAS-A relationship represents a "part-whole" association where one object acts as a component of another. 
    It is further divided into two types:
        1. Composition (Strong): The part cannot exist without the whole. For example, a House HAS-A Room. If the house is destroyed, the room is also destroyed.
        2. Aggregation (Weak): The part can exist independently. For example, a Department HAS-A Professor. If the department closes, the professor still exists as an independent entity.
